Introduction

Time-series data consists of observations collected over time, such as sensor measurements, financial prices, and activity logs. Increasingly, these signals are accompanied by text, images, event logs, and other contextual modalities. This creates richer learning settings, but also introduces challenges in temporal alignment, missing or noisy information, modality heterogeneity, scalability, and evaluation.

This tutorial presents recent advances in multi-modal time-series learning, spanning forecasting, reasoning, and question answering. We begin with problem formulation and multimodal forecasting with deep neural networks, then move to pretrained language models and time-series foundation models. Two guided, Colab-ready sessions provide practical examples for forecasting and question answering.

Topics

  • Problem definition, benchmarks, and emerging challenges in multi-modal time series.
  • Multi-modal forecasting: late fusion, auxiliary-variable alignment, and frequency-domain fusion.
  • Pretrained language and vision-language models for temporal reasoning and question answering.
  • Time-series foundation models and multimodal foundation models.
  • Cross-modal alignment, evaluation, robustness, scalability, and open problems.

Target audience

The tutorial is intended for Ph.D. students, researchers, and practitioners in time-series analysis, multi-modal machine learning, and AI applications. An intermediate machine-learning background and basic familiarity with PyTorch are assumed; prior time-series experience is helpful but not required.

Expected outcomes

  • Formalize multi-modal time-series tasks and identify their central modelling challenges.
  • Select appropriate fusion strategies and sequence backbones for paired time-series and text data.
  • Understand how language models and foundation models integrate temporal and textual signals.
  • Adapt the provided notebooks to new datasets and research problems.
